# References to add

## Executive summary

**What this is:** the references the revised paper needs, grouped by the section they support. Each one was checked against Crossref or the publisher on 2026-11-22, and comes with its DOI where there is one.

**Why read it:** the preprint cites six works. Reviewers at learning-analytics and computing-education venues will expect the assessment, learning-science and detection literature below, and will notice its absence.

**What it found:** every reference the review asked for exists, and the details are below. The two neuroscience figures in the preprint's Section 3 trace back to one 2026 review whose cited studies do not measure what the figures claim; they should be removed, not cited. Recent peer-reviewed keystroke-logging work (2024–2025) already detects non-authentic writing from process data. That is the closest prior work to GLP, and the paper has to position itself against it.

## The neuroscience figures: remove, do not cite

The preprint's "BDNF expression is upregulated as much as 2.8-fold during moderate cognitive challenge" and "dendritic spine formation increases by 37% under moderate cognitive load" both appear, almost word for word, in one source:

- Gkintoni, E., Sortwell, A., Vassilopoulos, S. P., & Nikolaou, G. (2026). Neuroplasticity-informed learning under cognitive load: A systematic review. *Multimodal Technologies and Interaction*, 10(1), 5. https://doi.org/10.3390/mti10010005

That review cites six papers for the two figures (Chen et al. 2021; Lampit et al. 2015; Romeo et al. 2021; Gangemi et al. 2023; Pozuelos et al. 2018; Weiss and Lavidor 2012). They are human MRI, EEG and brain-stimulation studies. None of their abstracts mention BDNF or dendritic spines, and spine formation cannot be measured in living humans with those methods. Neither number has a primary source.

If the paper mentions BDNF at all, it should also say that blood BDNF is a poor index of brain BDNF:
- Balietti, M., Giuli, C., & Conti, F. (2018). Peripheral blood BDNF as a biomarker of Alzheimer's disease: Are there methodological biases? *Molecular Neurobiology*, 55, 6661–6672. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12035-017-0866-y
- Naegelin, Y., et al. (2018). *eNeuro*, 5, ENEURO.0419-17.2018. https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0419-17.2018. This is a research article, not a review, and it states that no plausible mechanism explains how blood BDNF levels could reflect brain levels.
- For fairness, the counterpoint is Klein, A. B., et al. (2011), "Blood BDNF concentrations reflect brain-tissue BDNF levels across species." *International Journal of Neuropsychopharmacology*, 14(3), 347–353. https://doi.org/10.1017/S1461145710000738. That study used rats and pigs, not humans.

The simpler course is the one the revision plan recommends: leave molecular claims out entirely.
