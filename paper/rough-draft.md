# Process Evidence in Practice: Post-Grade Review of Consented Student Projects

**Rough study protocol · November 27, 2026**

Proposed only. No institutional approval or determination, recruitment, consent, data access, or results are reported here. This draft supersedes the earlier outline's proposed use during grading as the current working design; it does not replace any institutional application or policy.

## Abstract

Repository audits can surface observations about a finished artifact and the records available for its development. Their value depends on whether an instructor can verify the observation and interpret it without confusing a limited trace with a claim about a learner. We propose a consent-based retrospective study of the Frictional audit after grades have posted and no assessment can be affected. The instructor would compare descriptive judgments made from a permitted artifact packet with judgments after viewing a de-identified report, recording which claims changed, why, and what was unverifiable. This design studies interpretation and candidate usefulness in the reviewed sample. It cannot estimate an effect on grades, actual grading time, independent learning, or misconduct.

## 1. Introduction

Working software may demonstrate that a requirement was met, yet leave uncertain whether the visible trail represents offline work, a rewritten branch, collaborative decisions or a later import. A process audit can make visible evidence easier to inspect. It can also lend a misleading impression of authority to a sparse record. The [conceptual Frictional paper](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/frictional-measuring-the-struggle) limits the inference to support for a named claim. [Auditing the Making](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/auditing-the-making) proposes a synthetic benchmark of check accuracy and abstention. A classroom study must keep accuracy on fixtures separate from usefulness when a course's artifacts are incomplete.

Our first question is which report findings the instructor can confirm, cannot confirm, or can refute against permitted reference material. The second is which findings cause the instructor to revise a descriptive review note or suggest a different follow-up question, and whether the revision is supported. The third is how often missing evidence or common workflow confounds prevent confident interpretation. None of these outcomes comes from the actual course grading process.

## 2. Proposed design

**Scope and timing.** The proposed scope is four Spring 2027 course sections, subject to an approved protocol and adequate participation. No sample size or section-level coverage is assumed. Only consented projects selected under that protocol can enter a research dataset, after grades are posted. No research recruitment, data access or processing may begin until the institution provides its written determination and any additional approvals. No identifiable course artifact or raw student history is opened for this draft. Do not treat earlier inclusion targets or instruments in the repository as current approval.

**Separation of roles.** [ASSUMPTION: the institution approves an independent consent and de-identification process that keeps the instructor blind to participation through grading.] The instructor is the sole planned research investigator; any independent administrative support must be approved separately and receives only the access needed. Participation and withdrawal procedures, retention, support roles and any form of reuse must be settled before implementation. If grade-blind consent or source permissions cannot be arranged, do not start the study; revise the protocol or defer it.

**Evidence packets.** The starting packet describes the artifact and authorized evidence at a fixed snapshot; the second adds the Frictional report. The protocol must specify what a reviewer may check as a reference without seeing identifying information. Use a benchmarked release and retain its version and permitted configuration. Instructor familiarity with coursework or distinctive repositories can compromise de-identification in practice; measure recognition if permitted, suppress details, and narrow or remove any case that cannot safely be de-identified. No outside service receives identifiable, coded, or raw project materials. If any model-assisted analysis is permitted at all, send only approved anonymized numeric summaries.

**Two review passes.** The instructor records a descriptive observation and a plausible assessment question from the first packet, then revisits them with the report. Record additions, removals, changes, rationale and elapsed review time separately. The design's order is deliberate, so a second-pass change is a conditional observation, not an isolated causal effect of the report. Compare a second pass without the report only in a separately approved design. Do not reopen a grade, refer a learner for misconduct, or return a per-student score based on these research notes.

## 3. Measures and analysis

| Outcome | Unit and denominator | Handling unknowns |
|---|---|---|
| Verifiability | Findings judged supported, contradicted, or unresolved per project and dimension | Report all three and distinguish access limits from ambiguity |
| Supported additions | Project reviews with at least one independently checkable new observation / project reviews with both passes | Show if verification depended on otherwise inaccessible evidence |
| Unsupported changes | Reviewing notes where the report prompted an inference beyond the evidence / paired review notes | Label what was asserted and its alternative explanation |
| Review time | Minutes for each pass among observed pairs, reported separately | Research-review time does not represent grading time |
| Coverage and attrition | Counts eligible, consented, accessible, de-identified, audited and scorable | Do not publish small cells or reverse-identifiable examples |

**Table 1.** Proposed study outcomes. Freeze a marking guide for ambiguous findings and time measurements before reviewing data. If the protocol permits no second reviewer, report that the study cannot estimate inter-rater reliability. Analyze findings as clustered within projects, not as independent students. Summarize paired project changes descriptively with denominators and missingness; if the number of independent groups is too small, avoid overprecise intervals or comparisons by section. Report participant selection and workflow limitations; do not report an opt-in estimate as a course-wide rate.

## 5. Discussion and safeguards

A report may help by supporting a different follow-up question; it may harm by eliciting a confident inference from a partial trace. Both deserve reporting. Opt-in selection, order effects, instructor familiarity and a single-investigator design limit any generalization. The study does not validate a probability of genuine learning or distinguish human from AI authorship. Any future work on performance prediction needs a separate ethical review and an independently defined criterion.

Use only the authorized storage and access workflow if approval is granted. Honor exclusions where context itself could identify someone, record departures from plan, and keep manuscripts and public code free of student material. Keep the held-out portfolio analysis separate from this classroom protocol. Do not interpret this manuscript as permission to proceed.
