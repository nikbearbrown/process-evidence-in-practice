# Consent form (draft for the IRB)

> **Status (2026-11-22):** written for the earlier study design, which validated a learning score against a transfer check and used Fall 2026 records. Paper 2 is now a classroom study of the Frictional audit. What changes in this file is listed in [irb-protocol-notes.md](../irb-protocol-notes.md) §11.

## Executive summary

**What this is:** draft wording for the form students sign to let their course records be used in the Frictional research. There are two versions: one for students in next semester's courses (prospective, handed out by the third party in the last week of classes and sealed until grades are submitted) and one for students in past courses (retroactive, emailed after grades are submitted).

**Why read it:** the IRB will want the exact text students see. Northeastern's IRB has its own templates, so this draft is the content to pour into their template, not a finished form.

**What it decides:** participation is opt-in, collected at the end of the semester by someone other than the instructor, and cannot affect grades. Quoting a student's words and writing up a student's project as a case study are separate boxes, because both carry more re-identification risk than using numbers. The form also carries the elements FERPA requires for a written release of education records (which records, what purpose, to whom), so one signature covers both rules; Northeastern's guidance says a signed, IRB-approved consent form usually satisfies FERPA, and the Office of the General Counsel confirms. The IRB may decide a lighter form is enough for the exempt parts; let them say so.

Text in [square brackets] is to be filled in.

## Version A: next semester's courses (prospective)

**Research study: How learning shows up in the process of coursework when students can use AI**

**Researcher:** Nik Bear Brown, Associate Teaching Professor, [department], Northeastern University

**Who is asking you:** [name and role of the third party], who does not teach or grade this course. Forms are sealed until final grades are submitted. Your instructor will not see this form.

**What the study is about.** Assignments, essays and code no longer prove on their own that someone learned, because AI can produce them. This study asks whether the *process* of doing coursework (when you work, which mistakes you make and in what order, how sure you are of your answers, what you write in your Frictional log) carries information about learning that the finished work does not.

**What you are asked to do.** Nothing more. Every activity this study uses (quizzes with confidence ratings, spaced review quizzes, practice problems with hints, the Frictional log, the in-class check near the end of term) was part of the course for everyone, whether or not you take part. [If the full set ran: in this section, students were randomly divided into two groups that received review quizzes and hints on different topics, so that everyone received the same amount of each.] [If probes ran: the comprehension probes, short in-class exercises built from your own project code, were also part of the course for everyone. They counted for completion credit only and were never used to check academic integrity.] This form only asks whether your records from those activities may be used in research.

**Which records.** If you agree, the following records from [course number and title], [term] may be used:
- your FRICTIONAL.md log entries
- the commit history of your course work (times, commit messages and changes)
- your quiz answers and confidence ratings
- your scores on assignments, quizzes and the in-class checks
- [If probes ran:] your comprehension-probe receipts: which items you tried, how sure you were, how long each took, which hint you needed, and whether you were right. Not your answers, and not the probe sheets, which contain your code.

**Who will see them.** [Third party] removes your name, username, email address and other identifiers and replaces them with a code before the researcher sees any research data. The key linking codes to names is kept by [third party] only, and destroyed [when]. Records may be shared, without identifiers, with research collaborators listed in the IRB-approved protocol.

**Purpose.** Research on assessment and learning, published in academic papers and presentations.

**Your grade cannot be affected.** Your instructor will not know who took part. [Choose one, per the IRB: "Your instructor will never receive the list." / "Your instructor will receive the list only after final grades for the course have been submitted."] Saying no has no effect on anything in the course.

**Risks.** The main risk is that someone could recognize you from your work, especially because course work is posted in a public GitHub repository. To reduce this: results are reported as numbers and charts, not your work; distinctive project details are left out or changed; and your words are quoted only if you tick box 2 below, and only after checking that the quote cannot be found by searching the web.

**Benefits.** None to you directly. The study may help instructors find fairer ways to see learning than detecting AI use.

**Voluntary.** You may say no, or change your mind at any time before [date the data are de-identified] by writing to [third party email]. After that date your records can no longer be picked out, because the link to your name is destroyed.

**Questions.** About the study: [researcher contact]. About your rights as a research participant: Northeastern University Department of Human Research, IRBReview@northeastern.edu.

**Please tick what you agree to:**

- [ ] **1. Use my course records** listed above, without my name, in this research.
- [ ] **2. Quote my Frictional log,** with identifiers removed, in publications. (You may tick 1 without 2.)
- [ ] **3. Describe my project as a case study.** The description will be sent to me through [third party] before publication, and I may ask for changes or withdraw it. (You may tick 1 without 3.)
- [ ] **I do not want my records used.**

Name: ____________________ Signature: ____________________ Date: __________

(An electronic signature on the [third party]'s form counts, if the IRB approves it.)

## Version B: past courses (retroactive), sent after grades are posted

Same as Version A, with these changes:

- **Who is asking you:** sent by email from [third party] after final grades for [course], [term] were submitted.
- **What you are asked to do:** "You have already finished the course. Nothing more is asked of you. This form asks whether the records you created in the course may be used in research."
- **Your grade cannot be affected:** "Final grades for [course], [term] have already been submitted. Saying no, or not replying, has no effect."
- **If you do not reply:** "If you do not reply, your records will not be used in any way that identifies you. [Only if the IRB approves a (d)(4) secondary-use pathway: they may be included in counts and averages after identifiers are removed.]"
