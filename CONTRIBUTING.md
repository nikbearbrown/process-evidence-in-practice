# Contributing to Process Evidence in Practice

*Process Evidence in Practice* is a classroom study of the Frictional audit, planned for Spring 2027 courses under IRB review. Because it involves students, contributing here works differently from the other papers.

**Where it stands:** there is an outline and a set of IRB drafts. The IRB application has not been submitted, no student has been recruited and no student work has been opened.

**This repo is private.** It holds the study's instruments and its hypotheses, which students in the study courses should not see in advance. Access is by invitation from Nik Bear Brown.

## Who can do what

| If you are | You can | You cannot |
|---|---|---|
| **Anyone with access** | Review the outline, the measures and the wording of the instruments and consent materials. Check a reference. Point out a threat to validity. | See, handle or discuss any student's work, records or consent status. |
| **Study personnel** | Do the tasks the approved protocol assigns to you. | Do anything outside the approved protocol, or start before approval. |

To become study personnel you must be named on the IRB application and hold the human-subjects training the README lists. Ask Nik before assuming you are covered. Students enrolled in a study course cannot work on the study while they are enrolled.

## Pick a way to help

| Contribution | Where to start | What to send |
|---|---|---|
| **Review the study design** | [paper2-outline.md](paper2-outline.md) | A confound, an unlisted threat to validity, or a research question the design cannot answer. |
| **Review a measure** | [study/measures.md](study/measures.md) | Say what the measure would and would not show, and how it could mislead. |
| **Review an instrument** | [courses/instruments.md](courses/instruments.md) | Whether the wording is clear to a student, and whether it adds unreasonable work. |
| **Read the consent and recruitment drafts as a student would** | [irb/](irb/) | Any sentence that is unclear, or that could feel like pressure to take part. |
| **Check a reference** | The outline's related-work section | A primary source link, page or table, and the claim it supports. |

## Rules for this repo

- **Never put student data here.** No submissions, names, grades, consent lists or keys. The `.gitignore` blocks the folders that would hold them.
- **Don't share the instruments.** Do not copy items, probes or the study's hypotheses into any public repo, course site or chat.
- **Changes to IRB documents go through Nik.** Open an issue or a pull request; do not treat an edit as approved until he says so. After approval, any change may need an amendment.

## How to submit

Open an issue in this repo with the file or section, your evidence and a proposed next step. For an edit, branch and open a focused pull request.

## Credit and what we expect from authors

The author list isn't decided yet. We follow [Yale's guidance on authorship](https://provost.yale.edu/policies/academic-integrity/guidance-authorship-scholarly-or-scientific-publications). Under it, an author has done **all four** of these:

1. **Contributed to a component of the work:** its conception, design, conduct, analysis, or interpretation of at least part of the results.
2. **Helped write the paper:** drafted part of it, or revised it for substance, not just copy edits.
3. **Approved the final version** before submission.
4. **Agreed to be accountable** for the work, including helping answer questions about its accuracy or integrity.

Some contributions are valuable but don't by themselves make someone an author: a single issue, one checked reference, or a read-through of the consent materials. Everyone who contributes that way is thanked by name in the acknowledgments, unless they ask not to be. To be an author, take on a component, such as owning the analysis for one research question, or running a defined part of the approved protocol, and then do the writing, approval and accountability steps too. An AI model can't be an author, since it can't approve the paper or answer for it.

If you want to be an author, we expect you to:

- **Read the whole paper,** not just your section. You're putting your name on all of it.
- **Think about it, then give feedback.** Does the method answer the question? Are the comparisons fair? Do the numbers in the text match the tables, figures and data?
- **Suggest changes when they matter.** Don't make a suggestion just to have made one. If you find nothing to change, say so; that's a useful answer. A real problem, raised clearly with a proposed fix, is the most valuable thing you can give.
- **Disclose AI assistance.** AI tools are fine for code and drafting, but say where you used them. You stay accountable for everything you contribute.
- **Keep a record of what you did:** what, when, and where it lives (issue, pull request, commit, figure). The paper's contribution statement is drawn from it and lists each author's [CRediT roles](https://credit.niso.org/).
- **Sign off only when you can stand behind it.** Before submission, every author confirms in writing that they have read the full paper, agree with it, and accept accountability for it. If something is wrong or unclear, say so before approval, not after publication.

Raise authorship early, and we'll revisit it as roles change. Author order follows contribution. If you think your work merits authorship and it isn't reflected, say so before the final draft. Sending an issue or pull request does not by itself settle authorship.
